# Fact-check: "Cassandra - A Decentralized Structured Storage System" (Lakshman and Malik, Facebook; LADIS 2009)

Source: Avinash Lakshman and Prashant Malik (Facebook), "Cassandra - A Decentralized Structured Storage System". The paper prints no venue: its ACM footer is the placeholder "Copyright 200X ACM X-XXXXX-XX-X/XX/XX ...$10.00." (PDF p1). The story says `year: 2009` and authors "Lakshman and Malik" (two authors, same order as the title page).
Canonical file used: https://www.cs.cornell.edu/projects/ladis2009/papers/lakshman-ladis2009.pdf (6 PDF pages, created 2009-08-19).
Checked on 2026-11-12. The PDF text was extracted per page (p1-p6) and quotes were matched against that text.
Archive: not captured in this run (a PDF on the workshop site; no archive link recorded).

**Location convention.** The PDF has no section anchors. Links use `#page=N` (PDF page index; the paper has no printed page numbers). Locations give the paper's section number and heading plus the PDF page. The paper's own numbering is: 1 Introduction, 2 Related Work, 3 Data Model, 4 API, 5 System Architecture (5.1 Partitioning, 5.2 Replication, 5.3 Membership, 5.3.1 Failure Detection, 5.4 Bootstrapping, 5.5 Scaling the Cluster, 5.6 Local Persistence, 5.7 Implementation Details), 6 Practical Experiences (6.1 Facebook Inbox Search), 7 Conclusion. Note the paper's own roadmap paragraph (p1) is off by one against these headings; the headings were followed. Section 6 has no numbered sub-section for its bullets (7 TB load, failure detectors, Zookeeper), so those are cited as "§6".

**Result key.** `pass` = the claim as worded is in the paper, or is a plain restatement. `weakened` = the wording went beyond the paper; the Result column says what was done (fixed in the JSON). `scenario` = our invented example or metaphor, labelled illustration. `background` = not from the paper.

**What is in the paper:** Cassandra's design (consistent hashing with an order-preserving hash, N-way replication, Scuttlebutt gossip membership, the Phi accrual failure detector, commit log plus in-memory structure plus sorted data files with Bloom filters and compaction), the Inbox Search schema, and a few Facebook production numbers.
**What is not in the paper:** the words "memtable", "SSTable" and "doorman"; the notebook, whiteboard, filing-cabinet, desk, "worry meter" and "Pizza on Friday?" metaphors and scenario; the claim that "most" files are skipped; any guarantee that a Bloom filter answer is "definitely not" (it says only that the filter is "consulted to check if the key ... does indeed exist in the given file"). Also absent: any statement that Cassandra is eventually consistent (the paper says that of Bayou, Coda and Ficus in related work), and any statement that Cassandra took its ring from Dynamo (it cites Karger et al. [11] for consistent hashing and borrows only Dynamo's "preference list" name).

**Caution on "no boss" wording.** The paper claims "no single point of failure" (abstract) and calls Cassandra "a completely decentralized system" (§6), but §5.2 says "Cassandra system elects a leader amongst its nodes using a system called Zookeeper" that tells joining nodes which ranges they replicate, and §6 says "having some amount of coordination is essential". The story therefore attributes the no-single-point-of-failure claim to the paper and never says there is no coordinator.
